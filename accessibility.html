Accessibility

This page says what was actually tested on this site, what standard it was tested against, and where it still falls short. It is a statement of fact, not a claim of certification.

Target: WCAG 2.1 Level AA Standard: EN 301 549 v2.1.2 §9 1 open item

What standard, and why

This site targets WCAG 2.1 Level AA, tested through the web requirements of ETSI EN 301 549 v2.1.2, clause 9. Clause 9 §9.5 is the provision that incorporates WCAG 2.1 at Level AA, so the two are the same bar stated in two ways.

EN 301 549 is the harmonised European standard for accessible ICT. It is the reference that Canadian accessibility guidance points organisations to, which is why it is named here rather than only WCAG. Nothing on this page depends on being read in a particular jurisdiction: the measured requirements are the WCAG 2.1 AA ones.

How it was tested

By automated probes against the rendered page in a real browser, measuring the document the way a user agent computes it rather than reading the source:

  • Text and non-text contrast measured from computed styles, resolving oklch() to sRGB through the browser’s own parser so modern colour syntax measures correctly instead of silently returning nothing.
  • Reflow at 320 CSS px and at 200 % text zoom, checking for horizontal scrolling rather than assuming it.
  • Text-spacing overrides (line height, paragraph, letter and word spacing) applied to confirm no content is clipped.
  • Keyboard reachability, focus visibility, heading order, language, duplicate ids, and accessible names for interactive elements.

Contrast was measured against the composited background — translucent colours flattened over what is actually behind them — so the numbers below reflect what renders, not what the stylesheet intends.

On the contrast margin. The tightest pair in the palette now measures 5.02:1 against a 4.50:1 floor, up from 4.64:1. The fix was applied to the ground rather than the ink: --surface was darkened from 38 % to 35.5 % lightness, which raised every pair sitting on it at once — muted text (4.64 to 5.10) and all three tag fills (4.62–4.74 to 5.02–5.16). Lifting --muted instead would have cleared the threshold but left it 0.01 lightness from --fg, collapsing the two-tone hierarchy. Anyone editing the palette should re-measure rather than assume the margin survives.

Where it falls short

These are real, and stated plainly rather than rounded off:

  • No screen-reader pass. Everything above is machine-measured. No assistive-technology session — NVDA, JAWS, or VoiceOver — has been run against this site. That is the single largest gap in this statement: automated tools cannot judge whether a text alternative is accurate, whether reading order makes sense, or whether an interface is coherent when heard rather than seen.
  • Not everything is machine-checkable. Clause 9 runs to fifty requirements at Level AA. The table covers a subset. Requirements that need human judgement — whether alt text is meaningful, whether link text is descriptive in context, whether content is understandable — are outside what these probes can assert.
  • No conformance claim. A statement that this site “conforms to EN 301 549” would require a full audit, manual testing with assistive technology, and a documented evaluation. That has not happened, so no such claim is made here.
  • One page, one browser. Measurements were taken in Chromium. Behaviour in other engines, and with browser extensions or user stylesheets or forced-colours modes applied, is not characterised.
  • The share card image has no text alternative in situ. The social preview image at /img/og-card.jpg carries its content as painted pixels — the name, role and summary are drawn into the JPEG rather than being selectable text. Its meaning is conveyed to assistive technology only through the og:image:alt and twitter:image:alt attributes, which crawlers and social platforms may or may not surface. The same information is available as real text on this page and the profile page.
  • Level AAA contrast is not met, and is not targeted. This site targets WCAG 2.1 Level AA. The AAA threshold (1.4.6) requires 7:1, which the muted text tokens do not reach — they measure 5.02–6.95:1. Clearing 7:1 would require --muted at roughly 88 % lightness, which collapses it into --fg and removes the distinction the reading hierarchy depends on. Automated audits configured for AAA will report these as failures; that is accurate, and it is a bar this site deliberately does not claim.
  • Automated tools may misread the palette. The colour tokens are authored in oklch() and composited with color-mix(). Plain sRGB hex fallbacks are declared first for parsers that cannot read modern colour syntax, but a scanner that fails to parse color-mix() or oklch() may substitute a default background rather than reporting that it could not measure — producing a contrast failure that does not exist in any real browser. Verify reported failures against the rendered page before acting on them. The ratios quoted in this statement were computed from the tokens directly, with color-mix() flattened the way a browser composites it.
